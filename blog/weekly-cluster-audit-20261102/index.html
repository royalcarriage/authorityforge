Weekly Cluster Audit Checklist for Content Sites

A repeatable weekly audit so hubs and spokes stay intentional and monetizable.

To maintain intentional and monetizable topical clusters, perform a weekly audit by exporting index coverage data, mapping content ownership, removing or merging thin URLs, adding a new commercial spoke, and tracking impression changes. This routine ensures content stays relevant, indexed, and performs as intended.

Export index coverage

Start your weekly audit by identifying potential indexing issues within your clusters. Google Search Console (GSC) provides the most accurate data for this step. Focus on pages Google has discovered but not yet indexed, or those it has crawled but not indexed. These pages represent content that exists but isn't contributing to organic visibility.

Actionable steps: 1. Log into Google Search Console. 2. Navigate to Index > Pages. 3. Scroll down to the "Why pages aren't indexed" section. 4. Examine the following categories: * Crawled - currently not indexed: These pages were visited by Googlebot but deemed not ready for indexing. * Discovered - currently not indexed: Google knows about these URLs but hasn't crawled them yet. 5. Click into each of these categories. 6. Use the Export button (top right) to download the list of URLs to a Google Sheet or CSV for further analysis. 7. Filter these lists to focus specifically on URLs within your /systems/topical-clusters/ path. This narrows the scope to your target content.

Map hub ownership

A clear understanding of who owns which content within a cluster is vital for accountability and efficient updates. Without designated owners, content can become outdated, irrelevant, or orphaned. This step ensures every piece of content has a steward responsible for its performance and accuracy.

Create a master spreadsheet to track your clusters and their associated content. This spreadsheet acts as your central control panel for the audit.

Spreadsheet Columns: * URL: The full URL of the content piece (e.g., /systems/topical-clusters/content-auditing-guide/). * Hub URL: The URL of the main hub page this content belongs to (e.g., /systems/topical-clusters/). * Content Title: The title of the page. * Owner: The individual or team responsible for the content. * Status: (Live, Draft, Kill, Merge, Update Required). * Last Audit Date: When this specific piece was last reviewed. * Notes: Any specific actions needed or observations.

Example Row Entry:

| URL | Hub URL | Content Title | Owner | Status | Last Audit Date | Notes | | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------- | :------------------------- | :--------- | :----------- | :-------------- | :------------------------------------- | | /systems/topical-clusters/ai-writing-tools-for-seo/ | /systems/topical-clusters/ | AI Writing Tools for SEO | Jane D. | Live | 2024-05-20 | Needs update on new features. | | /systems/topical-clusters/keyword-research-tactics-2023/ | /systems/topical-clusters/ | Keyword Research Tactics | Mark S. | Update Required | 2023-11-15 | Outdated. Merge into main guide? |

Update this map weekly with new content, ownership changes, and status updates identified during the audit.

Kill or merge thin URLs

Thin content dilutes the authority of your clusters and can waste crawl budget. Identifying and addressing these pages is a core part of maintaining a high-quality content ecosystem. Your goal is to consolidate value and remove dead weight.

Definition of "Thin": * Pages with fewer than 300 words. * Pages with zero organic impressions in Google Search Console for the last 90 days. * Pages with no internal links pointing to them. * Pages that largely duplicate content found elsewhere on your site.
