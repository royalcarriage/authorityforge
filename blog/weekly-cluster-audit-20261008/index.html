Weekly Cluster Audit Checklist for Content Sites

A weekly cluster audit keeps your topical hubs and spokes intentional, ensures content remains relevant, and directly supports monetization goals by identifying performance issues and opportunities within your content architecture. This repeatable process helps operators maintain focus and efficiency.

Export index coverage

Begin by exporting your index coverage data from Google Search Console (GSC). Navigate to the "Pages" report under the "Indexing" section. Filter the data by your specific cluster subdirectory, for example, /systems/topical-clusters/. Export both "Indexed" and "Not indexed" pages as a CSV or Google Sheets file. This provides a baseline understanding of which cluster pages are visible to Google and which are not.

Concrete Steps: 1. Log into Google Search Console. 2. Select the property for your content site. 3. Go to "Indexing" > "Pages." 4. In the "Filter" bar, select "URL contains" and input your cluster path (e.g., /systems/topical-clusters/). 5. Apply the filter. 6. Export the data for both "Indexed" and "Not indexed" pages. Use the "Export" button, choosing "Google Sheets" for easier manipulation.

Map hub ownership

Maintain a clear, centralized map of all topical clusters and their responsible owners. This map should live in a shared spreadsheet (e.g., Google Sheets) and include critical information for each hub and its primary spokes. Assigning ownership ensures accountability and streamlines the audit process.

Required Spreadsheet Columns: * Hub URL: The canonical URL of the cluster's main hub page (e.g., /systems/topical-clusters/ai-content-generation/). * Hub Name: A descriptive name for the cluster (e.g., "AI Content Generation"). * Owner: The individual or team responsible for the cluster's performance and content. * Last Audited Date: The date of the last formal audit for this specific cluster. * Index Status (Hub): "Indexed" or "Not Indexed" based on your GSC export. * Key Spokes (URLs): A list or link to a separate tab detailing the primary spoke URLs within the cluster. * Notes: Any specific actions, issues, or opportunities identified.

Example Entry:

| Hub URL | Hub Name | Owner | Last Audited Date | Index Status (Hub) | | :-------------------------------------------- | :----------------------- | :--------------- | :---------------- | :----------------- | | /systems/topical-clusters/ai-content-tools/ | AI Content Tools | Content Team A | 2023-11-01 | Indexed | | /systems/topical-clusters/seo-reporting/ | SEO Reporting | Analytics Team B | 2023-10-25 | Indexed | | /systems/topical-clusters/content-audits/ | Content Audits | Content Team A | 2023-10-28 | Indexed |

Update this map weekly with index status from your GSC export and any new audit notes.

Kill or merge thin URLs

Identify and address thin, low-performing URLs within your clusters. These pages often consume crawl budget without providing value, or dilute the authority of stronger pages. Use GSC performance data (impressions, clicks) and content analysis (word count, unique value) to pinpoint candidates.

Criteria for "Thin" Content: * Fewer than 300 words. * Lacks unique insights or value compared to other pages. * Receives zero or very few impressions over a 90-day period. * Has no internal links pointing to it from other valuable pages.

Action Options for Thin Content:

| Action | Criteria | Impact | | :------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- | | Kill (410 Gone) | Content is completely outdated, serves no purpose, has no search demand, and cannot be improved or merged. | Signals to search engines that the page is permanently gone; frees crawl budget. | | Merge (301 Redirect) | Content has some relevant information but is too thin, duplicates another page's intent, or can enhance an existing, stronger page. | Consolidates authority and relevance onto a single, improved URL; passes link equity. |

Process: 1. Filter your GSC performance report for the cluster path. 2. Sort by impressions (lowest first) over the last 90 days. 3. Review pages with minimal or zero impressions. 4. Analyze the content of these pages for thinness or duplication. 5. Decide to Kill (implement a 410 status code) or Merge (implement a 301 redirect to a more relevant, robust page). 6. Document changes in your cluster ownership map.

Add one commercial spoke

Intentional growth requires identifying and filling gaps within existing topical clusters. Weekly, identify one opportunity to add a commercial spoke to a relevant cluster. This involves targeting buyer-intent keywords that align with your products or services and integrating them naturally into your content architecture.

Steps to Identify a Commercial Spoke: 1. Review your existing clusters and their associated keywords using a tool like Ahrefs or Semrush. 2. Look for "money keywords" or "buyer intent keywords" that are missing or weakly addressed within a cluster. Examples include "best [product]", "[service] pricing", "[product] vs [product]", "[product] reviews", "alternatives to [product]". 3. Ensure the keyword has sufficient search volume and commercial viability for your business. 4. Confirm there's a clear path to conversion (e.g., linking to a product page, a demo request form).

Commercial Spoke Checklist: * Target Keyword: Does it target a clear commercial-intent keyword (e.g., "best AI writing software for SEO")? * Audience Pain Point: Does the content directly address a specific problem a potential customer is trying to solve? * Solution Focused: Does it present your product/service as a viable solution? * Call-to-Action (CTA): Does it include a clear, prominent call-to-action (e.g., "Get a Demo," "Start Free Trial," "Compare Plans")? * Internal Linking: Does it link logically to relevant product
