Weekly Cluster Audit Checklist for Content Sites

A repeatable weekly audit so hubs and spokes stay intentional and monetizable.

A weekly cluster audit ensures content hubs remain focused and profitable by regularly reviewing index coverage, assigning hub ownership, eliminating thin content, integrating commercial spokes, and tracking performance metrics. This systematic process maintains topical authority and drives revenue.

A well-structured topical cluster acts as a pillar for your site's authority and organic traffic. Without consistent maintenance, clusters can sprawl, become diluted, or lose their commercial edge. This weekly checklist provides a repeatable procedure for hands-on SEO operators to keep their content intentional and monetizable.

Export index coverage

Start your weekly audit by understanding what Google sees (or doesn't see) from your clusters. This step identifies potential crawl budget waste and visibility issues early.

Open Google Search Console (GSC) for your property. Navigate to the "Index > Pages" report. This report shows which URLs are indexed and which are not. For a cluster audit, you'll want to filter this data.

Specific Steps:

1. Filter by cluster path: In the "Pages" report, click "New" under the "Page" filter. Select "URL starts with" and enter your cluster's base path, for example, /systems/topical-clusters/. This narrows the view to just your cluster's content. 2. Review "Not indexed" reasons: Examine the reasons provided for pages that are "Not indexed." Pay close attention to: * "Discovered - currently not indexed": Google knows about the page but hasn't crawled it yet. This could indicate crawl budget issues or low perceived importance. * "Crawled - currently not indexed": Google crawled the page but chose not to index it. This often points to quality issues, thin content, or duplication. 3. Export the data: Click the "Export" button in GSC and choose "Google Sheets" or "CSV." This allows for deeper analysis and tracking. Focus on the URLs listed as "Not indexed" within your cluster path. Prioritize URLs that are critical spokes or hubs.

This export becomes your working document for the week. Identify any hub or spoke URLs that are unexpectedly unindexed. These require immediate attention, either by requesting re-indexing (if the issue is resolved) or by diagnosing content quality problems.

Map hub ownership

Clarity on who is responsible for each content cluster prevents neglect and ensures strategic alignment. Every hub, and by extension its spokes, needs a designated owner. This person or team is accountable for the cluster's performance and adherence to the site's content strategy.

Use a centralized tracking system to maintain this ownership map. A simple Google Sheet can work, or you might integrate this into a more robust content planning tool like Notion or Airtable.

Key Data Points for Your Map:

* URL: The canonical URL of the hub or spoke. * Primary Keyword: The main target keyword for the content. * Assigned Hub: Which parent hub this content belongs to. * Owner: The individual or team responsible for the content's performance. * Last Updated: Date of the last significant content revision. * Next Review Date: When the content is scheduled for its next audit or update.

Process for Mapping:

1. List all cluster URLs: Use your GSC export or a content inventory to list every URL within your target cluster path. 2. Assign each to a hub: Verify that every spoke is correctly associated with its intended hub. This confirms the content hierarchy. 3. Designate an owner: For each hub (and implicitly its spokes), assign a clear owner. This owner is responsible for ensuring the content is up-to-date, performing, and aligned with commercial goals.
