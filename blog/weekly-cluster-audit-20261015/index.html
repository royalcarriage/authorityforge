Weekly Cluster Audit Checklist for Content Sites

A repeatable weekly audit so hubs and spokes stay intentional and monetizable.

A weekly cluster audit ensures your content hubs and spokes remain focused, Google-indexable, and actively contribute to your site's monetization goals. By systematically reviewing index coverage, refining cluster structure, eliminating underperforming content, adding targeted commercial pages, and tracking performance, operators can maintain topical authority and drive consistent revenue.

Export Index Coverage

Begin by understanding what Google currently indexes from your site. This step provides a baseline for your content's visibility and helps identify immediate indexing issues or unexpected inclusions. Focus on the actual indexed pages, not just what's submitted in sitemaps.

Exact Steps:

1. Open Google Search Console (GSC) for your domain. 2. Navigate to the "Pages" report under the "Indexing" section. 3. Locate the "Why pages aren't indexed" and "Why pages are indexed" sections. 4. Click on the "View data about indexed pages" link. 5. Click the "Export" button (top right) and select "CSV" to download the full list of indexed URLs.

This CSV file is your single source of truth for Google's current index. Cross-reference it with your internal content inventory to spot discrepancies. If pages you expect to be indexed are missing, investigate their status within GSC.

Map Hub Ownership

Intentional content clusters require clear structure and purpose. This step involves visually confirming the relationships between your hub pages and their associated spokes, ensuring each piece serves a specific role within the cluster. This prevents content overlap and strengthens topical signals.

Procedure:

1. Identify Core Hubs: List all primary hub pages (e.g., /systems/topical-clusters/). 2. List Spokes: For each hub, list all directly related spoke articles. 3. Confirm Internal Linking: Verify that each spoke page links back to its parent hub, and the hub links to its spokes. Use a tool like Screaming Frog to crawl your site and visualize internal linking if your site is large. 4. Define Purpose: For each spoke, briefly note its primary keyword target and its specific contribution to the hub's overall authority.

| Hub URL | Spoke URL | Primary Keyword | Monetization Goal | | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :-------------------------- | :---------------- | | /systems/topical-clusters/ | /systems/topical-clusters/audit-checklist | "content cluster audit" | Affiliate/Lead | | /systems/topical-clusters/ | /systems/topical-clusters/keyword-mapping | "topical map keywords" | Lead Gen | | /systems/topical-clusters/ | /systems/topical-clusters/ai-content-tool | "ai tool for clusters" | Affiliate |

This mapping process ensures every piece of content has a home and a clear reason for existence, preventing orphaned pages or redundant content.

Kill or Merge Thin URLs

Underperforming content can dilute your site's authority and waste crawl budget. This audit step focuses on identifying and addressing "thin" pages – those with low traffic, poor engagement, or minimal unique value – by either removing them or integrating their value into stronger pages.

Identification Criteria:

* Low GSC Performance: Pages with fewer than 10 clicks in the last 90 days (GSC Performance Report). * Low Word Count: Pages under 300 words (can be identified via Screaming Frog crawl). * Outdated Information: Content that is no longer accurate or relevant. * Duplicate Content: Pages that largely repeat information found elsewhere on your site.

Action Decision Table:

| Action | Criteria | Implementation | | :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | | Kill (301 Redirect) | Page has virtually no traffic, no backlinks, is irrelevant to your current strategy, or is a pure duplicate with no unique value. | Set up a 301 redirect from the thin URL to the most relevant, authoritative page on your site (often the parent hub or a closely related, high-performing spoke). If no relevant page exists, redirect to the category page or homepage. | | Merge (301 Redirect) | Page has some relevant information or a few backlinks, but is too short or weak to stand alone. Content can meaningfully enhance an existing, stronger page. | Copy valuable content and unique insights from the thin page into a target, stronger page. Update the target page's title, meta description, and internal links. Set up a 301 redirect from the thin URL to the strengthened target page. | | Update & Keep | Page has potential but is outdated or incomplete. It receives some traffic or backlinks and serves a distinct purpose. | Refresh the content with new information, expand its depth, improve its formatting, and add internal links. No redirect needed, but consider updating the publish date. |

After implementing redirects, monitor GSC for changes in index status and performance of the target pages.
