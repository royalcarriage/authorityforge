**Direct answer:** Picking affiliate tools for SEO sites requires promoting only software used in daily operations, placing clear disclosures above the fold, and grouping recommendations by actual operator workflows. This protects reader trust while generating steady referral commissions from genuine software demand.

## Criteria before links

Do not apply for an affiliate program just because the commission rate is high. If a tool fails your daily production checks, skip it entirely. 

Run every candidate tool through this verification checklist before inserting any tracking links:
- [ ] Tested on a live client site or personal property for at least 14 days.
- [ ] Exported actual CSV data from the tool to verify output quality.
- [ ] Contacted support with a technical question to measure response time.
- [ ] Checked affiliate payout terms (minimum payout threshold, holding period, and payment method).
- [ ] Confirmed cookie duration meets your traffic conversion window.

If a tool passes all five checks, it earns a spot in your content pipeline. If it fails even one, drop it and find a functional alternative.

## Disclosure placement

Readers need to know you earn a commission before they click your link. Burying a disclosure at the bottom of a 3,000-word post destroys credibility and violates FTC guidelines.

Place your disclosure directly beneath the H1 title or immediately before the first affiliate link in the text. Keep the language direct and transparent. 

| Placement Method | Reader Visibility | Compliance Level | Implementation Effort |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Top of page banner | High | High | Low (CSS/HTML snippet) |
| Above first button | High | High | Low (Markdown block) |
| Footer only | Low | Low risk / Non-compliant | Low |
| Sidebar widget | Medium | Medium | Medium |

Use a simple notice: "Transparency note: Some links in this guide are affiliate links. If you buy through them, I earn a commission at no extra cost to you." 

## Tool categories that convert

Focus your affiliate efforts on software categories that solve immediate technical and operational bottlenecks. Operators buy tools that save billable hours or uncover actionable data.

Target these four core categories in your content clusters:
1. **Log file analysis and crawling:** Tools like Screaming Frog or JetOctopus.
2. **Rank tracking and keyword research:** Tools like Ahrefs, Semrush, or Keywords Everywhere.
3. **Indexation and technical monitoring:** Tools like URL Monitor or IndexMeNow.
4. **Hosting and infrastructure:** Tools like Cloudways, Kinsta, or DigitalOcean.

Write tutorials focused on specific execution steps. Show how to configure a crawl or set up a log parser, then naturally link the software used in the demonstration.

## What not to promote

Protect your domain authority by avoiding low-quality products that generate high refund rates or spam complaints. One bad recommendation ruins months of trust building.

Never promote these specific offer types:
- **Generic AI writing wrappers:** Thin GPT API skins that output repetitive fluff without factual grounding.
- **Black-hat link networks:** Private blog network (PBN) hosts or automated spam tools likely to burn buyer sites.
- **Untested enterprise software:** SaaS platforms with high price tags that you have never personally logged into.
- **High-churn MLM SEO courses:** Overpriced coaching programs promising instant page-one rankings.

If you would not recommend the product to a direct client paying you an hourly retainer, do not recommend it to your readers.

## Weekly revenue review

Affiliate stacks require maintenance. Track performance weekly to spot broken tracking links, shifted cookie durations, or dropped commission rates.

Set up a recurring Friday review using this workflow:
1. Log into each affiliate dashboard (e.g., Impact, FirstPromoter, or direct SaaS portals).
2. Export click and conversion data into a master spreadsheet.
3. Check top-performing URLs in Google Search Console to ensure target keywords have not dropped in rank.
4. Update any software pricing changes or feature deprecations inside your published articles.

Maintaining accurate pricing and active links prevents user frustration and keeps conversion rates stable.
